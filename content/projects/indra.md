Indra is a reaction–diffusion medium: a grid of cells following local rules, with no objects stored anywhere. Structure isn't placed in it. Structure is what the field regenerates. The surprise, sixteen experiments in, is that nearly everything it does is measured by one ruler.

[Open the full-size overview](/images/projects/indra-overview-full.png)

## The arc so far

- **009 — Spiral-liquid.** A stable active medium self-organizes into spiral waves.
- **010 — Memory lives in the field.** Remove the spiral cores and they regenerate. The memory is in the underlying field, not the object.
- **011 — Wavelength measurement.** The spacing between cores sets a characteristic length: λ*.
- **012 — Memory fades without refresh.** Left unmaintained, memory decays with a half-life of about ten steps.
- **013 — The wavelength law.** λ* scales as 44·√D. One material constant.
- **014 — Topological stability map.** Engineered shapes — cavities, rings, channels, loops — are only stable above about 2λ*. Channels bead into chains below about 4λ*.
- **015 — Lamellar and beading.** Stripes are a real second phase, and thin channels break up by a Rayleigh–Plateau-style instability.
- **016 — Object interaction.** Cavities attract and coalesce when closer than roughly 12–13λ*. No bound state yet: bubbles, not atoms.

## One ruler

Spiral spacing, self-healing length, bead radius, the critical radius of a cavity, the minimum feature size — all sharp multiples of λ*. Lamellar wavelength and bead spacing fall in scaled bands rather than exact multiples. Change the diffusion constant and the ruler changes; everything else re-measures itself in the new units.

At high activity two phases coexist — spiral-liquid and lamellar — with a first-order, hysteretic transition between them. At low activity the medium goes quiet.
